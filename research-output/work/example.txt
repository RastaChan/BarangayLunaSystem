CHAPTER 4 
RESULTS AND DISCUSSION 
This chapter presents the results of the development, testing, and evaluation of the RFID-Based 
Attendance Monitoring System . It describes the implemented system modules, testing 
procedures, test cases, user evaluation, implementation plan, and identified risks. The results 
presented in this chapter are based on the actual functions implemented and tested in the 
completed system. 
4.1 Development and Testing 
The researchers developed an RFID-Based Attendance Monitoring System designed to improve 
the recording and monitoring of student attendance. The system addresses problems commonly 
encountered in manual attendance recording, such as time -consuming roll calls, inaccurate 
attendance records, duplicate entries, and difficulty in retrieving attendance information. 
The developed system uses Radio Frequency Identification (RFID) technology to identify students 
through their assigned RFID cards or tags. When an RFID card is scanned, the system retrieves 
the corresponding student information and records the attendance transaction in the database. 
The completed system includes functions for student registration, RFID card assignment, 
attendance recording, attendance monitoring, and attendance report generation. The system also 
records the date and time of attendance transactions, allowing authorized  users to monitor 
student attendance and retrieve attendance records. 
The system was subjected to functional, integration, system, performance, usability, and user 
acceptance testing, as applicable to the implemented functions. The testing focused on 
determining whether the system performed according to its specified requirements. 
4.2 System Modules / Features 
The completed RFID-Based Attendance Monitoring System consists of several modules that work 
together to automate the recording and monitoring of student attendance. 
Module 1: User Management 
PAGE 2
The User Management module provides secure access to the system through user login, 
authentication, account management, and role -based access. Administrators can manage user 
accounts and access privileges, while authorized users can access functions accord ing to their 
assigned roles. This module helps maintain system security and ensures that only authorized 
personnel can access and manage attendance information. 
Module 2: Student Management 
The Student Management module allows administrators and authorized personnel to add, update, 
search, and manage student records. It stores the information needed to identify students and 
associate them with their assigned RFID cards. This module supports t he accurate identification 
and management of students within the attendance monitoring system. 
Module 3: RFID Registration 
The RFID Registration module allows administrators or authorized personnel to register RFID 
cards and associate them with individual student records. It manages RFID identification numbers 
and their corresponding student information. This module enables th e system to recognize 
registered students during attendance scanning and ensures that attendance records are 
associated with the correct student. 
Module 4: RFID Attendance Monitoring 
The RFID Attendance Monitoring module automatically records student attendance when a 
registered RFID card is scanned. It identifies the student, records the date and time of the 
transaction, and handles duplicate scans based on the implemented system rule s. Students use 
the module by scanning their RFID cards, while teachers and authorized personnel monitor the 
recorded attendance. This module provides a faster and more organized alternative to manual 
attendance recording. 
Module 5: Attendance Records 
The Attendance Records module allows teachers, administrators, and authorized personnel to 
view, search, and filter recorded attendance transactions. Users can retrieve attendance

information based on available criteria such as student name or date range. This module 
facilitates efficient monitoring and retrieval of student attendance records. 
Module 6: Attendance Reports 
The Attendance Reports module allows authorized users to view and generate attendance 
information for monitoring and documentation purposes. It provides organized attendance records 
based on selected students, dates, or other available criteria. This modul e supports the 
preparation and review of attendance information for administrative and monitoring purposes. 
4.3 System Testing Procedures 
The researchers conducted several testing procedures to determine whether the completed 
RFID-Based Attendance Monitoring System operated according to its specified requirements. 
A. Unit Testing 
Unit testing was conducted on individual functions and modules of the system. The researchers 
tested functions such as user login, student registration, RFID registration, RFID scanning, 
attendance recording, searching, and report generation.  The purpose was to determine whether 
each individual function produced the expected result before being integrated with other modules. 
B. Integration Testing 
Integration testing was conducted to determine whether the different modules worked correctly 
when connected to one another. For example, when an RFID card was scanned, the system was 
expected to retrieve the corresponding student information and automatically create an 
attendance record in the database. 
C. System Testing 
System testing was conducted on the completed RFID -Based Attendance Monitoring System. 
The researchers tested the system as a complete application to determine whether its major 
components worked together according to the specified requirements. 
D. Performance Testing 
PAGE 4
Performance testing was conducted to determine the responsiveness of the RFID scanning and 
attendance-recording functions. 
The researchers measured the time required for the system to process an RFID scan and display 
or record the corresponding attendance information. 
E. Usability Testing 
Usability testing was conducted to determine whether intended users could navigate and use the 
system effectively. 
The evaluation considered aspects such as: 
• Ease of navigation  
• Ease of learning  
• Clarity of information  
• Ease of RFID attendance recording  
• Ease of retrieving attendance records  
F. User Acceptance Testing 
User Acceptance Testing (UAT) was conducted with intended users to determine whether the 
completed system could perform the attendance -related tasks required in the actual operating 
environment.

4.5 Results and Discussion 
The testing results demonstrated that the major implemented functions of the RFID -Based 
Attendance Monitoring System operated according to their expected behavior. 
The system successfully performed student registration, RFID card registration, attendance 
scanning, attendance recording, attendance searching, date filtering, and attendance report 
generation. 
One of the important findings was the system's ability to automatically record attendance when a 
registered RFID card was scanned. This reduces the need for manual recording and minimizes 
repetitive attendance entry. 
The testing also demonstrated the importance of duplicate -scan handling. During the initial 
testing, repeated RFID readings could result in duplicate attendance records. After the duplicate 
validation mechanism was implemented, the system successfully prev ented the identified 
problem during retesting. 
PAGE 8
The attendance search and date-range filtering functions also assisted users in retrieving specific 
attendance records. Instead of manually reviewing attendance sheets, authorized users could 
search for records based on the available filtering options. 
Based on the completed testing, the system addressed the identified problem of manual 
attendance recording by providing an automated RFID-based method of identifying students and 
recording their attendance. 
However, the researchers should recognize that successful functional testing does not 
automatically mean that the system is free from all possible errors. Actual performance may vary 
depending on the RFID reader, RFID card or tag, computer specifications, network/database 
configuration, and operating environment.

CHAPTER 5 
CONCLUSION AND RECOMMENDATIONS 
This chapter presents the conclusion and recommendations of the project based on the 
development, testing, and evaluation of the RFID -Based Attendance Monitoring System. It 
summarizes the project, presents conclusions based on the objectives and findings, and provides 
recommendations for the beneficiary and future researchers. 
5.1 Summary of the Project 
The project developed an RFID-Based Attendance Monitoring System  designed to improve the 
process of recording and monitoring student attendance. The system was developed to address 
problems associated with manual attendance recording, including the time required for 
attendance checking, possible recording errors, duplic ate entries, and difficulty in retrieving 
historical attendance information. 
The completed system provides several major functions, including user authentication, student 
management, RFID registration, RFID attendance scanning, attendance monitoring, attendance 
searching, date-range filtering, and attendance report generation. 
The RFID scanning feature allows registered students to be identified through their assigned RFID 
cards. Once a card is scanned, the system retrieves the corresponding student information and 
records the attendance transaction in the database. 
The system underwent functional, integration, system, performance, usability, and user 
acceptance testing as applicable to the implemented features. Initial testing identified issues 
related to duplicate RFID scans and attendance filtering. These issues we re corrected and the 
affected functions were retested. 
Based on the sample evaluation results, the system received a positive assessment from the 
evaluators. The findings indicate that the system can provide an automated approach to 
attendance recording and monitoring. 
5.2 Conclusion 
PAGE 14
The study was conducted to address the difficulties associated with manual student attendance 
recording and monitoring. Manual attendance processes can require considerable time and may 
make the retrieval and management of attendance records more difficult. 
To address these concerns, the researchers developed an RFID-Based Attendance Monitoring 
System capable of identifying registered students through RFID cards and automatically recording 
attendance transactions. 
Based on the system testing, the implemented modules successfully performed their intended 
functions. Student information could be managed, RFID cards could be registered and associated 
with students, and attendance could be recorded through RFID scanning. 
The testing process also demonstrated the importance of validating repeated RFID scans. An 
issue involving duplicate attendance records was identified during initial testing and was 
subsequently corrected. The affected function successfully passed retestin g after the corrective 
action. 
The attendance search, filtering, and reporting functions also provided users with a more 
organized means of retrieving attendance information. 
Based on the sample user evaluation and UAT results, the system was positively assessed and 
was able to perform the attendance-related tasks required by the intended users. 
Therefore, based on the presented findings, the researchers concluded that the developed RFID-
Based Attendance Monitoring System achieved its intended objectives of automating attendance 
recording, improving the organization of attendance information, and providing authorized users 
with tools for monitoring and retrieving attendance records. 
The conclusion should be revised if the actual testing or evaluation results do not support these 
claims. 
5.3 Recommendations 
Based on the findings, testing results, and identified limitations of the RFID -Based Attendance 
Monitoring System, the following recommendations are proposed. 
PAGE 15
A. Recommendations to the Beneficiary 
1. The beneficiary should consider adopting the RFID-Based Attendance Monitoring System 
to improve the efficiency of student attendance recording and monitoring.  
2. Authorized users should undergo proper training before using the system to minimize user 
errors and ensure that the implemented functions are used correctly.  
3. Student and RFID information should be validated  before assigning RFID cards to 
students to prevent incorrect attendance associations.  
4. Regular database backups should be conducted to minimize the risk of losing attendance 
and student records.  
5. The RFID hardware should be regularly inspected and maintained  to ensure reliable 
scanning and attendance recording.  
6. A backup attendance procedure should be maintained  in case of RFID reader failure, 
power interruption, or system downtime.  
7. The system should undergo regular maintenance and updates  to address technical 
problems and improve its reliability.  
8. User feedback should be collected periodically  to identify problems encountered during 
actual use and determine possible improvements.  
B. Recommendations to Future Researchers and Developers 
1. Future researchers may integrate multiple RFID readers  if the system will be deployed 
across several classrooms, entrances, or attendance areas.  
2. Future researchers may develop a web-based or mobile monitoring interface  that allows 
authorized personnel to access attendance information remotely.  
3. Future researchers may integrate SMS, email, or other notification mechanisms  for 
attendance-related notifications, subject to the institution's requirements and privacy 
policies.  
PAGE 16
4. Future researchers may improve the system's reporting and analytics capabilities  by 
providing graphical attendance summaries and student attendance trends.  
5. Future researchers may conduct larger-scale performance testing  involving a greater 
number of students and simultaneous RFID scans.  
6. Future researchers may explore offline synchronization mechanisms if the system needs 
to operate in environments with intermittent network connectivity.  
7. Future researchers may strengthen the system's security and privacy controls, particularly 
for protecting student information and attendance records.  
8. Future researchers may investigate the integration of the system with other school 
information systems , provided that data -sharing requirements, security, and privacy 
policies are properly addressed.
